# Quantum Physics

> **Depth:** overview | **Prior knowledge:** Some exposure to quantum physics concepts through videos and reading.

## Phase 1: The Quantum Revolution - A New Way of Seeing the Universe

This phase introduces the fundamental concepts that led to the development of quantum mechanics, highlighting the limitations of classical physics and the need for a new framework to describe the behavior of matter and energy at the atomic and subatomic levels.

- [ ] The Breakdown of Classical Physics — Discuss experiments like blackbody radiation, the photoelectric effect, and the discrete spectra of atoms which classical physics couldn't explain.
- [ ] Planck's Quantum Hypothesis — Introduce the idea that energy is quantized and comes in discrete packets called quanta, with energy proportional to frequency (E=hv).
- [ ] Wave-Particle Duality — Explain the concept that particles (like electrons and photons) can exhibit both wave-like and particle-like properties, demonstrated by experiments like the double-slit experiment.
- [ ] The Uncertainty Principle — Introduce Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle, which states that certain pairs of physical properties, like position and momentum, cannot be known with perfect accuracy simultaneously.

## Phase 2: Core Concepts of Quantum Mechanics

This phase dives into the essential principles and mathematical tools used to describe quantum systems.

- [ ] Wave Functions — Explain that particles are described by wave functions (Ψ), which contain all the information about the particle's state.
- [ ] Schrödinger Equation — Introduce the time-dependent Schrödinger equation, the fundamental equation that governs the evolution of quantum systems.
- [ ] Quantum Operators and Observables — Explain how physical quantities like position, momentum, and energy are represented by mathematical operators that act on wave functions.
- [ ] Quantum Superposition — The concept that a quantum system can exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured.
- [ ] Quantum Measurement — Discuss the process of measurement in quantum mechanics and how it causes the wave function to "collapse" into a definite state, introducing the probabilistic nature of quantum outcomes.
